Skew —
the targz language as hardware

The targz language in the skeuomorphic register — anodised chassis, backlit keys, jewel LEDs, engraved plates, knurled knobs. The things we ship, redrawn: layer dock, nav bar, page switcher, layer editor, viewport, Launchpad card, hatch bay. Anatomies are the real ones — the dock is lifted control-for-control from Portrait-Typo/ui.js.

Two rules: every button is a real button with a body, a skirt and travel, momentary or latching; every slider that sets a quantity became a knob, unless the value is a length, which stays a fader. One lamp, above left. Everything here works — press it.

Separate document. index.html is untouched and none of these tokens exist there. Section 10 is the bill for adopting it.

02 / The nav bar · momentary vs latching

The one idea flat UI cannot express. Import, Export and Console are momentary — they spring back, because they DID something. Reference, Light and Cells are latching — they stay down with the lamp on, because they ARE something. Today all six are the same grey icon. Press them.

momentary · latching · readout

03 / The page switcher · mechanical interlock

Three latching keys on one bar, one down at a time — the segmented control as the thing it has always been drawing. The key that is down is down: 4px lower than its neighbours, lit from inside, lamp bar under the engraving.

3-position interlock

04 / The layer editor · knobs instead of sliders

L1's Place tab. Every slider that set a quantity became a knob: 270° of travel, a lit arc, a pointer you read at a glance. Drag or wheel; shift for fine. One 48px strip — knob, engraved label, readout on a line — instead of the caption / knob / readout stack that spent 100px on one control. Zone from / to stayed a fader: that pair is a length, and a length wants a straight track — cut slot, engraved scale, waisted cap. Place from became a rotary selector, one pip per position, so you can count the choices without reading them.

rotary · 270° · shift = fine

05 / The viewport · gizmo, keypad, three knobs

The axis ball sits in a well under a glass dome — its six nodes are the six view keys, so you can hit a view from the picture or from the pad. Yaw / Pitch / Roll are knobs: three angles you dial, the pointer showing the angle literally.

Pan / Turn / Select moved out. The panel answers where the camera is; those three answer what the mouse does. Two questions, two objects — so they get a tool palette of their own.

axis ball · 6-key pad · 3 × rotary

06 / Launchpad · one rack module per project

The dashboard card as a rack unit. The running bar is a light pipe, the status dot a pilot lamp dark until the process is alive, the port a lit display, Launch / Stop illuminated pushbuttons with the lens inside the cap. Launch one and the whole module powers up.

07 / The hatch bay · a compound control

Five bare rows with a green number at the end said nothing about what the drawing would do. So the bay is built round a specimen, drawn at 1:1 mm. A ball rather than a swatch, because a rectangle of even hatch shows the spacing and hides everything else — and what hatching is for is tone. The blob is there because a fill can make good tone and still fall apart at an edge.

Three knobs, because a quantity is a knob and only a length keeps a track. They are independent — no control quietly edits another. Take spacing under the nib and the strokes overlap into solid ink: a fill, not a fault, and not reported as one. Every ink slot can be refilled, empty or not — a swatch you cannot change is a swatch lying about being a pen. The two cleanup thresholds sit in a recess, set once and forgotten.

two specimens · 1:1 mm · 6 fills · machine and hand

08 / Filling · by hand, and by machine

Two halves that want opposite things from one job: three fills that must not look like a machine made them, and the fill only a machine can draw. Same ball, same lamp, same holed blob — the only way the two can be read against each other. Below is the interface; the argument is in the code comments and HATCH-API.md.

A region is a function. tone(x, y) — negative outside, 0…1 inside, 0 bare paper and 1 as dark as the fill goes. That is the entire coupling: any subject that answers it can be filled by any generator here, and no generator asks what shape it is on. Everything is millimetres — convert at 96/25.4, and the paths go to SVG and the plotter with no rescale.

10 / The bill

What it buys, what it costs — about these components specifically, not about skeuomorphism in general.

What it genuinely buys

  • The colour bar becomes a light source. Edge-lit, it spills onto the metal beside it — a layer's ink is visible in peripheral vision, which a 3px flat bar never was. The one improvement I would miss.
  • On/off stops being a colour comparison. A lit lamp against a dead one is a brightness difference — readable without the row next door, and without colour vision.
  • Off-state honesty. A hidden layer has a dead pipe and a raised cap; today it is a 0.3 opacity dim you have to look for.
  • Destructive controls can be guarded materially — recessed, hazard stripes on hover — instead of relying on red alone.
  • Momentary vs latching is finally sayable. A key that springs back did something; a key held down is something. Today both are the same grey icon.
  • Knobs beat sliders for angles. The control's angle is the value, so you read Yaw / Pitch / Roll without reading any number.

What it costs

  • It is dark-only. Every effect here is light modelling against a dark body. The apps' canvas is paper-white, so the panel would sit on the drawing like a foreign object.
  • ~14 shadows per row against one background and one bar today. On a scrolling, dragging list that is real paint cost.
  • Text contrast fights the texture. 9.5px summaries on brushed metal are already marginal, and grain eats small type first.
  • It out-shouts the drawing. The current chrome disappears next to the plot. This does not.
  • Nothing else in the system speaks it. Tweakpane, the nav bar, the tab bar and the log would all need rebuilding — and section 04 is a hand-built Tweakpane, which the design system explicitly forbids.
  • Knobs are worse than sliders at three things: no drag-anywhere-along-the-line, no slam-to-the-end, poor on touch. Each needs a number box beside it — half a slider back.
  • Every knob is a pointer-drag handler. Tweakpane gives that away free, with keyboard, touch and value entry solved.
