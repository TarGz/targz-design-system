01 / The factories

CallWhat it is for
knob(…)A quantity. 270° of travel, a lit arc, a pointer. Takes detents:[…] to notch at places a value is approached deliberately, .set(v) so another control can move it, and one of five layouts (§05)
fader(…)A length, which is a straight thing, so it keeps a track. mode:'center' for a trim, because a trim is not "how much" but "how far off"
rangeFader(…)A span, the one thing a knob cannot say. Two jaws in one slot with the band lit between them — each jaw's mass points away from its own bar, so the ends can meet without ever overlapping
drum(…)A long list. Past about six names a rotary stops being aimable, so the names go on a real cylinder
keyBank(…)The same names as keys, mechanically interlocked — pressing one releases the others and the one that is down cannot be pressed up. A grid rather than a row, so nine of them is a block the size of a knob bay instead of 700px of bar. The control IS the list
gate(…)The same five-to-a-dozen names, with nothing that opens. A slot milled down the plate with a detent notch per position, a lever standing in it, the names engraved beside the notches. Everything legible at rest, and the only thing that moves is the lever
selector(…)Five to a dozen names — the gap between the two above: too many to aim at, too few to earn a cylinder. A window with the current name in it, opening to a list you can arrow through and type at. The current row is pressed IN and the pointed-at row lights: two marks, because a value list has a row that is already true before you touch it
lightDir(…)A direction, which is two angles and therefore not two controls. A flattened hemisphere: the angle round the disc is the azimuth, the distance from the middle is the elevation — centre is straight down, rim is the horizon. The numbers stay, under it, still typeable
rotary(…)A short list where every position must be visible at once. Hard ceiling of six: the fan is a fixed 80°, so detents fall from 27° to 9°
orbit(…)An orientation, and the only part here that sets one. Three cut arcs, each the great circle perpendicular to an axis: grab one and the ball turns about that axis, and the ring you are holding does not move, because a circle rotated about its own axis is the same circle. Roll the body for a free turn, throw it and it carries, double-click to come back to zero. Continuous where gizmo was discrete — it answers the question the six-key pad cannot: what if the angle you want is not one of the six
gizmo(…)A direction to look from. Six axes, so the picture is the control: you click the red node on the right rather than reading the word. No longer posed on this page — orbit took its tile, and the one panel that built an axis ball inline now builds an orbit ball instead. Still in the kit, still exported, and still the right part when the answer really is one of six named views rather than an angle
key(…) · pkey(…)Momentary did something, latching is something. pkey adds the mechanical interlock: pressing one pops the others out
.actrow + key(…, {cls:'act …'})A row of commands at the foot of a panel. Not a factory — two classes on the key you already have, and .act is the one that is easy to forget: .key is the surface, .act is the SIZE, and a cap without it is padding:0 at no height, so it shrinks to the width of its word and reads as text. One fire/go/halt verb at most, the rest aux, and an icon-only cap opts out of flex:1. §04
toggle(…)One boolean, with its name engraved beside the switch. The whole line is the button, and it carries .set(v) like every other value control. §06
swBtn(…) · chevBtn(…)The bare cap, for a row that already carries the name — and its mixed state, which is a master over a column. A knurled chevron for a thing you flick rather than press
typeable(el, read, write)Makes any lit window take a typed value on double click. A knob is for looking for a value; a keyboard is for knowing one
openPicker(…)Replaces input[type=color], whose popup is browser chrome no CSS reaches
assetRow(…)Load a file, and say what is loaded — thumb, name, source line, and a stack of small keys. The hidden input[type=file] is the reason it is a part: it cannot be styled, so it is owned and hidden here the way openPicker owns the colour input, and it resets after every pick so the same file twice fires twice. Empty is a real state — a well, a name that says so, and SAVE/CLEAR disabled. Three keys make the thumb 82px; two leave it at 56. §04
windowise(box, …)Makes a panel draggable and closeable. Close or reduce, never both
navBar({current, dock})The bar at the top of this page. §08
appDock({…})The other bar — the one an app runs on. All verbs, a fixed slot list and one readout. §09
menu(anchor, items)A .plate that opens off a key and knows how to close. §09
Two copies exist skew-kit.js and the inline copy inside skew-panels.html have diverged, and the axis ball that used to be the example is settled: that panel now builds orbit() out of the kit. It loads dist/ rather than src/ to do it — the built file is wrapped in an IIFE, so it can sit beside a page that already declares its own el without a duplicate-const throw. Its three angle knobs come from the kit for the same reason. The rest of that page is still its own copy, and every part of it that the kit has since grown is a part it will not get. One of them still has to become the other's import.

02 / Surfaces

the same lamp at 158° on all four
Top edge Lit. A 1px inset white hairline, because the top of a raised body faces the lamp.
Bottom edge Dark. A 1px inset near-black, the face turning away.
Specular Never centred. A highlight in the middle of a face reads as a sticker printed on it. Put it up and left, at roughly 34% / 28%.
Rotating parts A specular painted into a cap rotates with the cap. Relight it every frame against the fixed lamp, or give the part no orientation at all. A circle has none, which is why the rotary's lever became a round knob.
Spheres The opposite, and it looks like the same bug. A polished dome shows no shading change as it turns: only surface features move, and a dome has none. Sliding its gradient reads as the surface deforming.
Proud, topLit hairline inside the top edge.
Proud, bottomDark hairline inside the bottom, plus a skirt and a cast shadow.
Recessed, topHard near-black inside the top edge, walls shading down from it.
Recessed, bottomFaint light inside the bottom, the one wall the room reaches.
Recessed, outside A lit lip below the cutout, and no cast shadow at all. A hole does not throw one. This is the line that decides it.
In one token --recess is all four walls and the lip. --chamfer is the broken edge, concave: dark at the top, bright at the bottom. Backwards and the control reads as a bump, every time.
.stage Neither, and it is the panel these specimens are posed on. A chassis is a raised body, a well is a cut into one, a stage is a field: the same case metal at low light, a hairline instead of a bevel, one soft radial off the lamp so it is not flat. Nothing bolts to it and nothing cuts into it — things are set down on it. Every specimen on this site sits on one, and so does the drawing area of an app

05 / Knob layouts

all five, live · drag, wheel, shift for fine, double-click the window
layoutShape · and when
stack Vertical, bare metal. Caption, dial, value in a column, 46px. The default, and what three side by side need: a row of dials has to be named before it can be read.
tile Vertical, proud plate. The same column on its own raised body, so it is a module rather than a control. What a grid wants, because the plate is the edge that says where one channel stops and the next begins.
row Horizontal, proud plate. Dial, caption, value on one line, 40px. A column of them reads as a rack of channels. This is what the settings drawer is built from.
compact Horizontal, bare metal. Dial and value, no caption, 34px. A strip earns its plate by being one of several in a column, and a lone control in a row that already names it does not.
bare Neither. The dial alone, for when the value has its own window somewhere else, which is what the pen bank does: the height is lit into the key's own face and the dial only turns it.
Do not strip a stack Before these existed, two callers built a stack and deleted .klab and .kval by hand. That is a caller that breaks the day the markup changes, and it silently keeps the stack's 46px and its column gap.
Orthogonal size, detents, bipolar, fmt, arc and .set() all work in every layout. Only the default size changes with it.

06 / The toggle

three candidates, then three places it lives · throw them
Reach forWhen
toggle({label})The default, and it comes out as a slide switch: a ribbed actuator in a slot milled through the faceplate, thrown left or right. The legend is the POSITION, so there is nothing printed on it and nothing to invent — you read it from the far side of the room without reading anything
toggle({icon})When the boolean has an honest glyph: an eye for a preview, an outline for the outlines. Then it is the backlit cap, silkscreened, exactly as the layer dock builds it. An arbitrary glyph is worse than none — an eye on a switch with nothing to do with seeing — and a blank cap is worse than both
toggle({lamp})When the switch belongs to a layer. The colour lights the pipe down the leading edge and the floor the actuator uncovers, and it is the layer's own ink. Leave it off for a plain setting: a pipe on every boolean is a column of light saying nothing, and the pipe is the one signal here that carries across a room
swBtn(…, 'mixed', …) on a .row.allA master over a column. Down means "this is happening" and the colour says how completely — the accent for all of them, white for some. It needs the column under it to mean anything, which is why it is a bus bar and not a toggle: a different material, once, for the one switch that commands the others. And it needs a glyph, because a slide has no third position and inventing one would be a mechanical answer to a logical question
The gate goes first in the bayA boolean that turns a whole bay on is not a setting among the settings it governs. It sits directly under the bay cap, above everything it gates, and it carries the bay's lamp. Nothing below it moves or greys — see the declined row
.row + swBtnWhen the thing IS a module: contents, a colour, an editor and a chevron to open it. That is the layer dock, and it is on PANELS §01. There is no card here, because a card is a claim a boolean cannot back up
key(…, {latch:true})A toggle on a bar of keys, where its neighbours are momentary. It matches them and it stays down; the hatch bay's ALL is one
chevBtnNot a toggle. It shows and hides content. It carries no value, and nothing downstream reads it
.piano · rotary · drumNot a toggle. Two NAMED states are an interlock (§07): "Portrait / Landscape" is not "portrait: on". A switch is right when the off state is genuinely the absence of the thing, and wrong the moment the two states both have names
Open — three dresses, one survives The same call and the same markup; A and B are the same 46×22 and C is its own shape. They differ in where the light and the material are. A .slide puts the material in the actuator: a knurled moulded grip, ribs, a specular streak, and the colour is a floor behind it. B .slide.pill inverts that — matte charcoal actuator with a dimple grip, and the lit window becomes the whole object with the engraved end of the slot showing through it. C .slide.rocker stops being one object in a row: it is a pilot LAMP, a SWITCH and an ENGRAVED NAME stacked in a column, the way a console mounts one, with a paddle whose half that is OUT catches the lamp and throws a shadow into the well, and whose half that is IN shows no edge at all. A is the quietest; B reads from furthest away; C is the only one that reads as a real control — and it costs 42px of height, three parts, and a bank instead of a list. A column of nineteen is a different panel from the one this app has. Two are deleted, not kept as options: an option is a decision nobody made.
Not a lamp, and the capsule is B's alone The blank cap was first answered with a lamp behind a lens in the middle of it, which is wrong twice. It says "lit" where this panel already has jewels, pipes and lit arcs saying that, and it turns every switch on the plate into a dot. The slot is a rounded rectangle for the matching reason: at a capsule radius the actuator stops being a machined part and becomes a dot in a pill, which is the shape every operating system draws for this — B takes that radius deliberately and pays for it with the glow and the engraved ends, which is a different argument, not the same one made twice.
swBtn is REBUILT, toggle is not swBtn bakes its state into the class, the aria and the title at construction, so flipping a class by hand leaves the title saying "Show" while the lamp says on. It has to be replaced wholesale, and its container has to be in ENGAGE_ROOTS for engage() to find the replacement and light it. toggle repaints in place instead, which is what lets it carry .set(v) like every other value control — and .set() does not fire the handler, because a config load is not a user pressing nineteen switches.
Declined — the gated bay does not dim A gate turns off two knobs and neither of them greys, so the panel still offers a clearance to a weave that is not running. It is not built because no app does it yet, and a rule this page states but the language does not implement is drift wearing a hat. It is filed.

08 / The nav bar

view mode · nothing navigates
VIEW A
FieldContract
Adding it <script src="skew-nav.js"></script> as the first node in <body>, and id="main" on the content wrapper. The module writes the skip link, the sentinel and the dock itself. That is the whole integration.
Two modes mode:'doc' loads documents: anchors, aria-current, no tablist, four tab stops. mode:'view' swaps a view inside one page: buttons, a real tablist, roving tabindex, arrow keys, and nothing navigates. The question that picks one is whether the thing behind the key is a document or a view.
Registry One PAGES array in that file. The bar and the home's cards both read it, so adding a document is adding an entry and the two cannot disagree about what exists.
Parts .strip chassis, .demo-etch.nav-mark wordmark, .chan milled divider, .piano.nav-pages switcher, .lcd.nav-ver version window. Every one already existed. The bar adds a dock and a wordmark size, and nothing else.
Anchors, not buttons Destinations are <a class="pkey nav-link">. A <button> with an onclick that sets location looks identical and loses middle-click, cmd-click, the status bar preview and copy-link. .pkey is a look; the anchor is the mechanism.
Current page A <span>, not an anchor: class="pkey nav-link is-down" plus aria-current="page". It keeps the 2px of travel, the recess and the lamp, and has no href because a link to here goes nowhere.
Tablist, but only one way In doc mode there is no role="tablist" and no roving tabindex: these load documents, and four anchors are already four correct tab stops. In view mode it is the opposite and it is a real tablist, because those keys reveal panels already in the document. The two common bugs are one bug in two directions: a router faking links with click handlers, or a view switcher reloading the document to change a panel.
The dock eats clicks The dock spans the viewport and the bar does not, so the dock carries pointer-events:none and the strip puts them back on for itself. Skip it and you have an invisible full-width lid over every page.
Skip link Four destinations in front of the content on every page is four tabs before the first heading. .skip is off screen until focused, then lands on the metal like anything else, and targets #main.
Seated state .navdock.seated deepens the cast shadow and adds a 1px outline once the page has scrolled. One IntersectionObserver on a 1px sentinel, never a scroll listener: two callbacks in the life of the page instead of sixty a second.
Position position:sticky; top:14px. Sticky and not fixed, so the bar starts in the flow and no page needs a padding kept in step with the bar's height.
z-index 40 nav · 60 skip link · 80 floating plate · 99 room grain · 200 / 201 off-canvas drawer and its tab. The nav sits under the grain on purpose: the grain is the room, and the room is over everything.
Version window Read from version.js by dynamic import. A number written into two files drifts on the first commit that only remembers one, and a failure here is a .dim window rather than a dead script.
Under 620px The wordmark, the dividers and the version window go. The destinations stay: they are the only load-bearing part of a nav bar.
Not in scope No actions, no search, no menu. A bar carrying both destinations and verbs makes you read it to work out which is which. Verbs belong on the panel that owns them.

09 / The app dock

live · the menus open, nothing is saved or exported
READY
FieldContract
Not the nav bar §08 carries destinations and refuses verbs — "a bar carrying both makes you read it to work out which is which". This is the other half of that sentence: a bar that is all verbs, plus one readout. An app has both, and they are different objects. Destinations in one bar, verbs in the other, never both in one.
Adding it document.body.append(appDock({…})). Every argument is a handler; there is no argument for which keys exist. fixed:false takes it out of position:fixed and leaves it in the flow, which is what the specimen above is doing.
The slots, and all of them FILE ▸ Load All · Save All · New  ·  EXPORT ▸ PNG · SVG · debug SVG  ·  IMAGE  ·  LIGHT  .chan  SOUND · RESET WINDOWS · CONSOLE  ·  READOUT. In that order, in every app. An app that ships a subset ships a different bar, and then the one piece of chrome carried between nine apps stops being the same object. What varies between apps is what the handlers do.
Export carries three rows PNG is what it looks like, SVG is what the pen will cut, and the third is what the app thought — the same drawing with the working geometry left in. On a generative tool that is the only file that answers why did it do that. It takes no shortcut and it is not optional: an app that reaches its debug output by editing a constant and reloading has a debug output nobody looks at.
The channel Left of it is everything that touches the drawing — load it, export it, the reference under it, the light on it. Right of it is the workspace: the sound, the windows, the console. It is not a split between momentary and latching, which is why a verb sits between two states without reading as a mistake.
Momentary against latching Read per key, off the lamp: a key lit while nothing is touching it is holding something open or on; a key that flashes and springs back did something. FILE, EXPORT, IMAGE, LIGHT and RESET WINDOWS are verbs. SOUND is the only state on the bar — FILE, EXPORT and CONSOLE are doors, and they carry aria-expanded where SOUND carries aria-pressed.
The console key shows the console It does not turn one on. Press it and the log is up, press it again and it is gone, and no state is left behind either way — it is the same object as the two menus with a different plate on it. Wiring it as a latch is the easy mistake, because a door and a state look identical for as long as the plate happens to be open.
The console The readout with a history, so it is the same green monospace behind the same glass: the .lcd says how many strokes are on the sheet now, the log says what the app has been doing about it. dock.log(text, lit) — lit for a line that is a result (what got made, how many, how long), plain for a note. The app pushes whether the plate is up or not: a log that only records while you are watching is not a log.
RESET WINDOWS is the way back Mandatory for the reason windowise gives: a window dragged mostly off screen has no handle left to grab, and folding does not help because the head goes with it. An app that lets a panel be moved and carries no way to put it back has a state a user cannot get out of. The mark is four panes, not a grid — what you press it for is that picture.
One lamp, one icon A latched key says its state with the lamp, not with a second glyph — §06's rule for switch caps. ICON.soundOff exists for a switch that has no lamp; a key has one.
The menu openPlate(anchor, body) is the mechanism the plate never had — one open at a time, Escape, click-outside, and a flip rather than a clip at a window edge. menu(anchor, items) is that plus rows and an arrow walk; the console is that plus a log. It opens upward, because this bar is on the floor.
The key stays lit For as long as its plate is open. On a bar of seven icons that lamp is the only thing saying which one you are in — the plate carries no title and needs none, because it is anchored to the key and the key is the label. That is also why no plate here has a tail: two channels already say where it came from, and a pointer on the edge only tells the truth while the plate is directly over its key — a wide one never is, since it slides to fit the window. And the key that opened it closes it: without that, clicking the lit key tears the plate down and builds an identical one in the same place, which reads as a control that does nothing, so you try it twice.
A chip is bound or it is a lie The factory binds ⌘/Ctrl+O S P E itself, because a row printing ⌘S that does not answer to it is worse than a row with no chip. A row with no shortcut leaves the column empty — New has none, and giving it one to square off the right edge is inventing a key binding for the sake of a straight line. shortcuts:false is for a page showing a dock rather than running one; this page passes it, or ⌘S here would stop being the browser's.
The readout An .lcd, never a button. It is a fact about the drawing and the app pushes it: dock.count(16844). Nothing inside the dock counts anything.
The dock eats clicks Same trap as §08 and it costs more here: the dock spans the viewport and the bar does not, so without pointer-events:none on the wrapper you have an invisible full-width lid across the bottom of the canvas — which on a drawing app is the strip of paper nearest the hand.
It is chassis Like the strip in PANELS 09 it cannot be closed, reduced or dragged. A canvas whose only route to Save can be put in a corner is a canvas you can lose.
Parts .strip chassis, .key caps, .chan between the two groups, .lcd readout, .plate menu. Every one already existed. What is new is the placement and the mechanism.

11 / The room and the sheet

a square unit, and the panel that is not the work
6×

the rules for this sheet are in the settings drawer

SurfaceWhat it is
The desk --room #0b0b0d, plus one radial from high centre so the top of the page is lit and the bottom falls away. The lamp of §02 lands somewhere, and this is the somewhere. A flat fill reads as a void; a gradient reads as a room
The air body::after — the grain over everything at opacity .035 and mix-blend-mode: overlay. Fixed, not absolute, so it does not swim when the canvas pans: it is the air in the room, not a texture on the panels, and a grain that travels with the artwork is a grain the eye reads as dirt on the paper
.sheet White, two shadows, and nothing reaches inside it: no grain, no tint, no filter, no blend mode. The shadow is ten times a panel's throw, because a white sheet on a near-black desk at panel numbers reads as a sticker. .window drops it, for a 3D view or a depth map: a window onto something is not paper on a desk and does not throw
.grid The only thing on the sheet that is not the work, which is the whole difficulty: readable enough to align against, faint enough that a rule is never mistaken for a stroke. Two weights, majors and sublines, and the sublines are thinner and fainter — below about 0.3mm a line stops getting lighter as it gets thinner, so alpha has to carry the hierarchy. Every number is a custom property, because every one is a setting the Grid bay writes
.ruler-tick Engraved on the desk, so light lines. This is the one place the engraving rule inverts: everywhere else a cut is darker than its surface, but the desk is already near black and a near-black tick on it is not a tick
Trap
Set --grain-z or the haze lands on your panels It defaults to 99, over everything, which is right here because this page has no floating panels. An app has them, and the grain belongs under them and over the canvas — Portrait-Typo runs 90 against panels at 96 and up. One line in your own :root. Leave it and you get a haze on your own panels, which is an afternoon spent looking for it in the panel's CSS.
The canvas is a hole, not a panel Nothing in the language paints inside it. Any glass, reflection or dome over a <canvas> needs a wrapper, because a canvas has no pseudo-elements, and the reflex of reaching for ::after fails silently.
A drawer must narrow the canvas, not shove it Typo insets #canvas-container with right: var(--push) rather than translating it, so the container genuinely narrows, the 3D canvas re-measures and the sheet re-centres in what is left. The rulers take the same inset or they measure the window and call it paper. A translateX also breaks any panel dragged by getBoundingClientRect(), which includes transforms: grab a panel with the drawer open and it jumps sideways by the drawer's whole width.

12 / The orbit ball

live · grab a ring, or roll the ball itself · three sizes, one state
FieldContract
orbit() — the ball, and the default An open bore: socket, ball, collar. Three cut arcs, each the great circle perpendicular to one axis. Grab one and the object turns about that axis — the ring stays put and the surface travels round under your hand. Roll the body for a free turn, throw it and it carries, double-click to come back to zero. This is what a panel gets unless it says otherwise, because glazing changes how the socket lights and how much of the ball you can read — not something that should arrive because somebody upgraded the kit.
orbit({ glass:true }) — the dome, asked for The same ball under a glass cover cut to the plate's hole, with clearance so nothing touches the collar. Spherical to brk, then a flat flange; the two bright rings are its two corners, and the shell's second surface a wall-thickness in. It does not turn: the ball rotates underneath it.
Same API, either way orbit({ size, yaw, pitch, roll, glass, onChange, onHover }), and .set({…}) silent · .turn({…}) animated · .get() · .live(key, driving) · .glass(on). onChange({yaw,pitch,roll}, axisKey). orbitBay wraps a ball and its three dials and forwards all of it.
Three angles out, a matrix in the middle The panel stores yaw, pitch and roll, so three angles is what it hands back. What it does during a drag is a matrix, because the axis a ring turns about is wherever that ring is pointing and that is not one of the three the angles are written in. Euler-as-state gimbal-locks at pitch ±90; a matrix has no poles.
The glass contains the light A dome over the bore is a lid, so the plate's light does not arrive on it: no wash on the collar while an axis is driven, no bottom highlight in the well, no pool under the ball. The feedback moves inboard — the arc, the well lips and the ball's underside still say which axis is live. All of it scoped, so an unglazed ball is unchanged.
The glass is geometry, not lighting It never turns, so it is baked in buildGrid beside the normals and a drag reads one number per pixel. The price: editing ORBIT_GLASS — the dome's material, shared by every glazed ball — does nothing until somebody calls reglaze(). The bench below does.

13 / Traps

background resets The background shorthand wipes background-image. Restating a state with only the colour layer strips every other layer off the one element you are looking at. This file has been bitten twice, once erasing a knurl, once a specular.
clip-path clips shadows A clipped silhouette loses its box-shadow entirely. Use filter: drop-shadow(), which follows the shape, which is what a shadow does anyway.
Canvas has no pseudo-elements Any glass, dome or reflection over a <canvas> needs a wrapper div. Not obvious until the reflection silently fails to appear.
SVG inner rotation Rotating a child rather than the whole <svg> needs transform-box: view-box. Rotating the whole svg instead turns its lighting with it, which is §01's problem wearing a different hat.
Rebuilding a list under an open popup Filling a slot from inside a picker's callback redrew the rail, throwing away the button the open picker still pointed at. Every later drag then wrote to a detached node.
Substring selectors .pencap.on is a substring of .pk-well .pencap.on. A scripted edit anchored on the short one sliced out a whole CSS section, and the verification check then matched the same wrong rule and reported it fine. Anchor to line start.

Flush is about panels, not everything --raised and --sunk are still live: a cap stands proud of its bay and a pressed cap cuts into it. A control is an object you push; a bay is a surface you do not.
No dim tier on accent Measured, secondary ink on #FF6A00 drops below 4.5:1 the moment it is dimmed at all — the floor is 87% strength. A caption on an accent face is either full ink or unreadable. If a bay needs a caption tier, it is not an accent bay.
The leader.lead with .over, .side, .rail or .gutter. A skin themes three values — --lead-line, --lead-ink, --lead-lit — and nothing else. .on makes a group live.
CapsA cap with no material is made of its bay's. Contrast is the exception — .m-dark, .m-pale, .m-accent on the key itself, for the two or three genuinely consequential keys on a panel.
Stack vs splitA .stack is two keys glued into one module and gives two presses; a .split is one key whose legend names a pair or a range. Reach for the stack when the halves do different things, the split when they are one thing with two ends.
A split cap must not be .skirt The divider is .split::before and the chamfer is .key::before. One element, one ::before — the divider is the later rule and wins, so the cap silently loses its chamfer and nothing tells you until the pressed state looks flat.
No backlight on a beige key The dark theme lights a latched legend because a black cap 2px down in a black panel is invisible. Here the shadow does that job, and a lamp drawn on a surface nobody believes is backlit is the sticker failure with a legend on it. A latched light cap is down, not lit — except a dark one, which loses its edges to its own shadow and keeps its lamp.
A live leader is not the brand orange At 1px, #FF6A00 measures 1.88:1 against the room — a live group nobody can see is live. --accent-line is the accent darkened until a stroke clears 3:1. It is for marks only; a face or a cap still uses --accent, or the panel has two oranges.
Two tinted bays, one edge Never butt them. A chassis rib or a seam goes between. With the z-fork gone this stopped being advice and became the mechanism — the seam is now the only thing telling you where one bay ends and the next begins. The rib between them is --rib, and it is a real surface rather than spacing: under about 16px it stops reading as the case showing through and goes back to looking like a fat border.
