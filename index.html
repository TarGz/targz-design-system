Skew
the targz language, machined

One question, asked twice: if the targz visual language were built as hardware, anodised chassis, backlit keys, jewel LEDs, engraved plates, what would the apps look like? Each document takes a real app, rebuilds its anatomy out of machined parts, and says what that buys and what it costs.

The parts are shared. physical-ui.css carries every primitive both documents use, and each document adds only what is its own. A language is only a language if the second thing you build with it is mostly parts from the first.

Nothing here is adopted. The shipping design system is old/index.html, which is untouched and is the third card below, and so is every app these explorations read from. Skew is a proposal you can operate, which is the only honest way to judge one.

the same builder · dock off
FieldContract
Markup <nav class="navdock"><div class="strip nav-strip">…</div></nav>, preceded by <div class="nav-sentinel"> as the first node in <body>. Both are required: the dock positions, the sentinel reports.
Parts .strip chassis, .demo-etch.nav-mark wordmark, .chan milled divider, .piano.nav-pages switcher, .lcd.nav-ver version window. Every one of them already existed. The bar adds a dock and a wordmark size.
Anchors, not buttons Destinations are <a class="pkey nav-link">. A <button> with an onclick that sets location looks identical and loses middle-click, cmd-click, the status bar preview and copy-link. .pkey is a look; the anchor is the mechanism.
Current page A <span>, not an anchor: class="pkey nav-link is-down" plus aria-current="page". It keeps the 2px of travel, the recess and the lamp, and has no href because a link to here goes nowhere.
Not a tablist No role="tablist", no roving tabindex. These load documents; they do not switch panels inside one. <nav aria-label> plus anchors is already the right thing, and anchors are tabbable without help.
The dock eats clicks The dock spans the viewport and the bar does not, so the dock carries pointer-events:none and the strip puts them back on for itself. Skip that and you have an invisible full-width lid: the top row of every page below stops responding and nothing on screen says why.
Seated state .navdock.seated deepens the cast shadow and adds a 1px outline once the page has scrolled. Driven by an IntersectionObserver on the 1px sentinel, never a scroll listener: one callback at the crossing instead of one per frame.
Position position:sticky; top:14px. Sticky and not fixed, so the bar starts in the flow and the page does not need a padding that has to be kept in step with the bar's height.
z-index 40 nav · 80 floating plate (the picker) · 99 room grain · 200 / 201 off-canvas drawer and its tab. The nav is deliberately under the grain: the grain is the room, and the room is over everything.
Tokens --led for the key that is down, --grain for the strip texture, --font for every legend. The version window is .lcd phosphor and takes .dim when the version fails to load, because a display reading nothing is powered, not dark.
Registry One PAGES array builds the switcher and the cards in 01. Adding a document is one entry. It lives in this page's script today and moves to a shared module the first time a second document carries the bar.
Under 620px The wordmark, the dividers and the version window go. The switcher stays: the destinations are the only load-bearing part of a nav bar.
Reduced motion The seat transition is the only animation in the component, and prefers-reduced-motion removes it.
Not in scope No actions, no search, no menu. A bar that carries both destinations and verbs makes you read it to find out which is which. Verbs belong on the panel that owns them.
